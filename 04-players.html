6  The major business players

This chapter maps the field. For each company the questions that matter for investing are: how big is it, how AC-focused is it (a little or a lot of the business), what is its share of the industry, and is it levered to the high-growth parts (commercial, applied, data center, premium efficiency)? Figures below are approximate first-pass numbers — the financial chapter and later deepening will tighten them against primary filings.

6.1 The global leaders by AC volume

The global air-conditioning equipment industry is led by a handful of names that recur across rankings: Daikin, Midea, Gree, Haier, and Carrier, with Mitsubishi Electric, LG, Samsung, Johnson Controls, and Trane close behind.1 Asia-Pacific is the center of gravity, accounting for roughly 47% of industry revenue.

6.3 The cleanest “AC growth” exposures

Daikin Industries — The world’s largest air-conditioning maker, operating in 170+ countries, with AC as essentially its whole business. It reported record results for the fiscal year ended March 2025 (~¥4 trillion / ~€29 billion in net sales), led by applied and commercial AC and by strength in India and Japan (Daikin Industries, Ltd., 2025). It is the purest large-cap bet on the global cooling story, but trades in Tokyo and at a premium.

Carrier Global — Spun out of United Technologies in 2020, Carrier has reshaped itself into a pure-play HVAC company: it acquired Viessmann Climate Solutions (European heat pumps) and divested its Fire & Security and commercial-refrigeration businesses (Carrier Global Corporation, 2025). That makes it one of the most direct U.S.-listed ways to own residential-plus-commercial cooling and the heat-pump transition. Reported revenue is roughly $20+ billion.

Trane Technologies — A pure-play climate company, more commercial- and applied-weighted than Carrier, plus the Thermo King transport-refrigeration franchise. ~$22.5 billion revenue (Trane Technologies plc, 2025). Frequently the market’s preferred “quality” name in the group; the question is always valuation.

Lennox International — The most concentrated North American play (~$5.2 billion revenue), spanning residential, commercial, and refrigeration, with a 2024 ductless/VRF joint venture with Samsung extending its product range (Lennox International Inc., 2025). Smaller and U.S.-centric, so more exposed to the domestic replacement and refrigerant cycle.

6.4 The “AC is a small part of a giant” names

Mitsubishi Electric — The Air Conditioning Systems & Home Products segment generated roughly $9.4 billion in FY2024 and the company is a global VRF/ductless leader (Mitsubishi Electric Corporation, 2024) — but that segment is only one part of a sprawling industrial conglomerate (factory automation, power, semiconductors). You get genuine AC leadership diluted by unrelated businesses.

LG and Samsung — Both are serious in ductless/VRF and (for LG) data-center cooling, where LG cited ~$720 million of regional orders in 2024 — but AC is a modest slice of each company’s enormous electronics revenue. Exposure is real but diluted.

Johnson Controls — Pivoting away from equipment toward commercial building solutions, controls, and service; it agreed to sell its residential and light-commercial HVAC business to Bosch for $8.1 billion (Johnson Controls International plc, 2024). After that, it is more a “smart buildings / service” story than an AC-equipment story.

Vertiv is the cleanest data-center-cooling adjacency and is profiled in full — business, segments, growth, backlog, and risks — in the data-center cooling chapter, alongside Modine and nVent. For European growth specifically, the best-positioned listed names are Carrier (via its Viessmann heat-pump acquisition), Daikin, NIBE (Stockholm), and Mitsubishi Electric, with Bosch and Vaillant relevant but private — see the market chapter.

Note

Investor takeaway. If the goal is concentrated exposure to the cooling-and-efficiency growth story via liquid public equity, the shortlist is short: Daikin (global, purest, Tokyo-listed), Carrier and Trane (U.S.-listed pure-plays), and Lennox (North-America-concentrated). Vertiv is the cleanest data-center-cooling adjacency. The conglomerates (Mitsubishi, LG, Samsung, JCI) and the Chinese volume leaders (Midea, Gree, Haier) round out the picture but each comes with a “what am I really buying” caveat.
