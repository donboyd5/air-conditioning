9  Picks and shovels: refrigerants and components

Some of the most interesting investable ideas here are not the equipment makers. The forced low-GWP refrigerant transition and the rising technical content of systems create demand for two upstream layers — refrigerant chemicals and components (especially compressors and valves) — where a few firms have real pricing power and where genuine technological change and new leaders are emerging. This is the “picks and shovels” angle you asked to develop.

9.1 Layer 1 — Refrigerant chemicals

The AIM Act (U.S.) and F-Gas Regulation (EU) are forcing the switch from high-GWP R-410A to low-GWP A2L refrigerants (R-454B, R-32) and HFO blends. That is a multi-year demand surge for the underlying fluorochemicals — and the foundational HFO patents are concentrated in two hands.

9.1.1 Solstice Advanced Materials (NASDAQ: SOLS) — the cleanest pure-play

The single most important development for this thesis: Honeywell spun off its Advanced Materials division as Solstice Advanced Materials, which began trading on Nasdaq as SOLS on October 30, 2025 1. The refrigerant/fluorine franchise that used to be an immaterial slice of Honeywell is now a stand-alone stock. FY2025 net sales were ~$3.9B, of which refrigerants and applied solutions were ~72% (refrigerants alone ~$1.3B). Crucially, in February 2026 the European Patent Office upheld Solstice’s foundational HFO-1234yf process patent against challenges from Arkema and Chinese producers — its HFO IP portfolio runs into the mid-2030s 2. SOLS is the most direct “refrigerant transition” play.

9.1.2 Chemours (NYSE: CC) — more torque, but PFAS liability

Chemours sells Opteon HFO refrigerants in its Thermal & Specialized Solutions segment (~31% of revenue). The growth is real — FY2025 TSS sales hit a record ~$2.1B (+13%), with Opteon volumes up ~56% on the AIM Act transition 3. But Chemours carries large, partly uncapped PFAS (“forever chemicals”) liabilities: a ~$450M DOJ settlement, an ~$875M New Jersey settlement (over 25 years), a share of a ~$1.185B water-systems settlement, and unresolved personal-injury and natural-resource claims 4. So CC offers the most direct Opteon volume torque but you underwrite an open-ended legacy liability. SOLS is the cleaner play; CC is the higher-beta, litigation-encumbered one.

9.1.3 Who owns the pricing power — and the sector-wide risk

Solstice (Honeywell) and Chemours jointly dominate the foundational HFO patents and license them to others, which has historically supported pricing power. Other suppliers are more diluted: Arkema (Euronext: AKE) sells R-454B (Forane) under a Honeywell arrangement; Daikin (6367.T) is both the #1 AC maker and a major fluorochemicals/R-32 producer; Orbia/Koura (BMV: ORBIA) and Chinese producers (e.g., Zhejiang Juhua, 600160.SS) round out supply but are diluted or access-constrained.

WarningThe “is the moat permanent?” risk: TFA

HFO refrigerants degrade in the atmosphere into TFA (trifluoroacetic acid), an ultra-persistent compound now under intensifying scrutiny in Europe (2025 reports from PAN Europe and CHEM Trust). If regulators classify TFA as a problematic “forever chemical,” the HFOs themselves could eventually face restriction — a structural long-term risk to the entire HFO franchise (SOLS, CC, Arkema). This is the single biggest question mark over the refrigerant-chemicals thesis and worth tracking. It also keeps natural refrigerants (R-290 propane, CO₂) in play as a longer-term alternative.

9.2 Layer 2 — Components, especially compressors and valves

The compressor is the “engine” of every system, and valves are its nervous system. This layer is interesting because the technology is changing (oil-free magnetic-bearing compressors, propane-ready parts) and because EV thermal management is pulling the same component makers into a second, faster-growing end market.

9.2.1 The compressor map: mostly private, one big IPO to watch

The marquee names are largely private: Danfoss (Danish, incl. the Turbocor oil-free compressor), Bitzer (German), Tecumseh, Bristol. The most important development is Copeland — the former Emerson Climate Technologies, the global scroll-compressor leader, carved out by Blackstone (~$14B EV) and rebranded. Copeland confidentially filed for a U.S. IPO in November 2025 5 — it is the single most important pending listing in this space and would, for the first time, give public investors direct access to the “heart of the system.” Not yet investable; watch the IPO.

Compressor exposure available through listed parents includes Midea (000333.SZ) — owns GMCC/Welling, the world’s largest AC-compressor maker; Nidec (6594.T / NJDCY) — owns Embraco; plus LG (066570.KS) and Panasonic (6752.T).

9.2.2 Sanhua (002050.SZ / 02050.HK) — the standout listed component play

The most investable pure-ish component name is Zhejiang Sanhua Intelligent Controls. It holds >50% global share in electronic expansion valves and four-way valves — the two most critical AC valves — with FY2024 revenue ~RMB 28B (~$3.9B, +14%) and accelerating (H1 2025 net profit +39%) 6. The growth engine is EV thermal management: Sanhua has been a key Tesla thermal supplier since 2016, bridging its AC-valve franchise into autos. It completed an “A+H” dual listing in mid-2025 (Hong Kong, raising ~$1B), which improves access for international investors versus pure mainland A-shares. Its main valve competitor, Zhejiang Dunan (002011.SZ), is the #2 and also plays EV thermal.

9.2.3 Where the technology is genuinely changing (and who leads)

  • Oil-free magnetic-bearing compressors — Danfoss Turbocor pioneered these (now ~30 years old, >140,000 installed, up to ~40% more efficient than screw compressors, and available on low-GWP HFO-1234ze). A real efficiency edge, riding data-center chiller demand — but Danfoss is private.
  • EV-thermal crossover — the biggest “upstart-to-leader” dynamic: AC-component makers (Sanhua, Dunan, and Chinese specialists like Aotecar) scaling fast on EV thermal content. Sanhua is the cleanest listed way to own it.
  • Propane (R-290) / natural-refrigerant-ready components — a fragmented, mostly-private OEM space today; the component beneficiaries (A2/A3-rated valves and sensors) again point back to Sanhua/Dunan.
