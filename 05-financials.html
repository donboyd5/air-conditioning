8  The financial lens

This chapter populates the comparison you asked for across a five-name shortlist — Carrier (CARR), Trane (TT), Lennox (LII), Daikin (6367.T), and Vertiv (VRT) — using the latest reported fiscal year and a dated market snapshot. An Excel version of this table is in the project as ac_comp_sheet.xlsx for sorting and extending.

WarningAs-of dates — read this first

Operating figures are the latest reported fiscal year: FY2025 (ended Dec 31, 2025) for the four U.S.-listed names; fiscal year ended March 31, 2026 for Daikin. All market-based figures (price, market cap, P/E, EV/EBITDA, yields) for the U.S. names are as of June 26–28, 2026; Daikin’s are as of May 2026 and are distorted by an activist (Elliott Management ~3% stake) situation. Multiples move daily — re-pull before acting. Sources: company filings/IR and stockanalysis.com (S&P Global / Fiscal.ai).

8.2 How to read it

Trane (TT) is the quality benchmark: the best margins (18.6% EBIT) and returns (25.7% ROIC) among the diversified names, the lowest leverage (~1.0× gross debt/EBITDA), strong free-cash conversion, and a heavy recurring-service tilt (>40% of revenue is recurring/service per trade-press estimates). You rarely buy it cheap — ~26× EV/EBITDA, ~31× forward earnings.

Lennox (LII) is the cheapest and highest-quality-per-dollar of the building-HVAC names: the highest operating margin (20.1%), highest FCF yield (3.4%), lowest EV/EBITDA (18.7×) and forward P/E (22.6×). The trade-offs are slower growth (−2.7% in a down shipment year), the smallest scale, ~100% North American concentration, and full exposure to the U.S. refrigerant/replacement cycle.

Carrier (CARR) is the most “in transition”: revenue fell as divestitures (Fire & Security, commercial refrigeration) washed through, and it carries the highest leverage (3.8× gross debt/EBITDA) after the Viessmann heat-pump acquisition. Its FY2024 GAAP numbers are distorted by ~$4.5B of one-time divestiture gains — use FY2025. The thesis is a cleaner pure-play HVAC + heat-pump company once the balance sheet de-levers; the ~7% ROIC needs to rise to justify the multiple.

Daikin (6367.T) is the purest large-cap cooling bet (~92% AC) and, on these numbers, the cheapest (~10× EV/EBITDA, ~24× forward P/E) with the lowest leverage — but also the lowest margins (8.3% EBIT), a yen-translation overlay, and an event-driven valuation right now (Elliott activist stake disclosed April 2026). Cheap-looking, but margin and governance are the questions.

Vertiv (VRT) is a different animal: 28% revenue growth, the best ROIC (32%), and a huge AI-driven backlog — at the price of the richest multiple in the group (~49× EV/EBITDA, ~45× forward P/E) and the highest beta (2.0). It is a data-center-infrastructure growth stock, not a building-HVAC compounder. Detail in the data-center cooling chapter.

Note

Investor takeaway (not a recommendation). The group sorts cleanly: Trane = highest quality, full price; Lennox = best value + margins, but small and U.S.-cyclical; Daikin = purest and cheapest, lowest margins + FX/governance overlay; Carrier = transition/de-leveraging story; Vertiv = high-growth AI play at a high-growth multiple. Which fits depends on whether you want defensive compounding (TT/LII), global pure-play exposure (Daikin), a turnaround (CARR), or AI-cooling growth (VRT). For a 10–15 year horizon, the durable-quality + reasonable-price intersection is where I’d focus the next round of work.
